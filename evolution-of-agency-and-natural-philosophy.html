Natural Agency

Evolution of Natural Agency

Agency predates contemporary AI. Agency in natural organisms is the most generalized organizational framework within which individuals formulate and produce their actions (Evolution of Agency, Tomasello, 2022). Agents decide for themselves what to do according to their own best judgment in a given situation. Types of psychological agents in evolutionary order of emergence are shown in the Table.

Each new form of psychological organization represented increased complexity in the planning, decision-making, and executive control of behavior. Each also led to new types of experience of the environment and, in some cases, of the organism's own psychological functioning, leading ultimately to humans' experience of an objective and normative world that governs their thoughts and actions.

Table. Agents in evolutionary order of emergence (Tomasello, 2022)
Agent Type Architecture Direction of actions and attention Controlling of Actions
Goal-Directed Agents: Lizards, 300 mya Feedback Control Systems Goals Attention to relevant situations Global Inhibition of Go/No-Go decisions
Intentional Agent: Squirrels, Rats; 250 mya Additional Executive Tier with working memory Intentions as Plans to Goals Attention to goals and actions Monitoring uncertainty in Either-Or decisions
Rational Agent: Great apes, Chimpanzees; 20 mya Additional 2nd order Executive Tier Plans for future goals Attention to executive decision Diagnosing problems and intervening to make "better" decisions
Normative Agents: Human groups 6 mya; Societies 150 kya Social self-regulation of executive tiers Obligation Shared Goals with meshing plans Perspective flexibility Social-normative self-regulation via rational and moral norms

Agent Functions

Agents: Functions and Operations

Agent types are distinguished by the functions they perform (Norvig) which are visible through behavior or statements of the agent's developer.
